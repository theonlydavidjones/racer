Tactics, leg by leg: mock-up

A still mock-up for the owner, before building. It shows where the tactical debrief would sit in the results' "Your race, debriefed". The numbers come from one sim race: Seaweed Soup course 2, 14 kt westerly, an ebb, six boats, measured from the race's own tracks and current. The seconds marked ≈ are rounded estimates for the layout. Each beat and run gets its lines ranked by what cost the most. Each line is one plain sentence with a number (− lost, + gained, against the fleet's middle on that leg). No new buttons: the same section appears in "Watch your race", where each line jumps to its moment.

How each is measured, and the standard behind it:

  • Shifts: time on the headed tack, from each boat's own wind against her 1-minute mean. Seconds lost at the sim's own rate: cos(β+θ)/cos(β−θ), as the existing debrief uses. Coaching: on oscillating shifts tack on the headers, on persistent ones get to the side the wind is moving toward (Sailing World, "Windshift Rules of Thumb"; Dave Perry on shifts). No published share of "time on the lifted tack" was found, so the sim compares you with its own fleet.
  • Laylines: metres past the layline, and the extra distance they cost. Coaching: approach late, tack on the layline even when you fear overstanding, overstand only as insurance (Dellenbaugh, "Five familiar layline dilemmas", Windcheck).
  • Side: your mean distance off the leg's line against the fleet's, and whether the boats on your side did better. Leverage and consolidating (Dellenbaugh, Speed & Smarts) still to be quoted from the sources.
  • Tide: the current along the leg under each boat, from the sim's NOAA-based current field. On a beat the current helping you up the course counts as a gain; on a run, against you as a loss. General rule: the beat in the most favourable or least adverse current. Local relief sources (Latitude 38, StFYC) not yet reachable, so no Bay lore is quoted. The numbers are the current model's.
  • Clear air: seconds lost in another boat's shadow (the existing finding, now per leg). Coaching: the shadow runs about 5–10 lengths along the other boat's apparent wind, longer in light air (Dellenbaugh, "Light and Heavy Air Differences"; Windcheck, "Don't lurk in wind shadows").
In "Watch your race"

In the replay, under each leg's row in "Against …", a Tactics list holds the same lines. Picking one plays that stretch. A tide line marks the current under you and under the fastest boat on the leg, a shift line the stretch you spent headed, a layline line the overstand.

Built from what's already recorded (the 1 Hz track with each boat's wind shift), plus one new field per frame: the current under each boat. The results-page section sits after "What cost you time" and "Against the winner".
