# Weather helm in the sail band (r035): four options, mock-ups, nothing built

The owner, on the first mock-up: "the white text can't be read easily. I don't want the measurement showing at the top. I
feel like it belongs in the control panel. show me some options." So, in the band at the bottom (stills in `band/`, each
option on both tacks; `_close` crops the band's middle). In every one: the tiller now 6.5° to windward, its average over
the last few seconds 4° (her weather helm), a well-balanced 2–5° to windward in teal, and amber past 8° (option c's port
tack shows 9°). Windward swaps with the tack: starboard tack to the right, port tack to the left.

- **a. A helm ring** round the dial's bottom, the heel ring's partner: the dot is the tiller now, the hollow ring its
  average, the teal arc the balanced band. Its degrees go with the point of sail under the dial. Crowded: the point of
  sail has to move down onto the mainsheet row.
- **b. A helm rail**, a row under the traveler drawn as the band's tracks are (P … S): the tiller as the car, its average
  a hollow marker, the teal band to windward, and the degrees ("4° W", dark on light) at its end. Display only: no
  arrows, nothing to drag.
- **c. Just the number**, beside the point of sail: "Close reach · 4° W", in the band's own label; amber past 8°.
- **d. The tiller on her own deck** in the dial: the tiller drawn from the rudder head (angles ×3 so they can be seen), a
  ghost at its average, the teal wedge where a balanced helm lies; the degrees on hover.

Sources (rules of thumb, not a textbook): Good Old Boat, "the rest of the ratios on helm balance": "between 2 and 4
degrees of weather helm for optimal performance upwind"; "above 8 degrees ... too much weather helm". A sailing forum
gives 3–5 as ideal and about 10 as too much. Nothing specific to the Folkboat was found. The band is an ESTIMATE.
