# Josephine: what the owner has confirmed

Facts about the boat's look that the owner has stated or confirmed. Check new drawing work against this list before
showing it, and add to it whenever the owner confirms something. One line each; note the photo where there is one.

## Hull
- The bow's profile, from the stemhead to the bottom of the keel's leading edge, is one long, flat, gentle S: no straight
  stem, no corner just below the waterline. (Haul-out photos; the lines diagrams.)
- The bow is slim below the water; above the water its topsides are rounded, not flat.
- Clinker planks keep nearly their width end to end, tapering only slightly; they don't come to a point at the bow.
- The stem is straight-raked above the water (not convex).

## Deck and rails
- The deck and cabin top are non-skid: a paint with crushed walnut shells (a fine irregular grit), laid in a field
  that stops about 2 in short of every edge, leaving a smooth gelcoat margin (along the toerail, round the cabin's foot,
  the cockpit, the forehatch and the mast; on the cabin top in from its edges and round the sliding hatch). The margin
  is the bare fiberglass, a warm white gelcoat, not painted. Its edges are crisp.
- One toerail, continuous and unbroken along the whole deck edge. No second rail or rubbing strake on the topsides.
- The hiking rail (where the crew sit to hike out) lies inboard of the toerail, alongside the cockpit, the cockpit's
  length (cabin's after end to the coaming's after end). Not alongside the cabin.
- The coaming doesn't change shape at the cabin's after end.

## Cabin
- The cabin roof is a slice of a cone lying on its side, narrowing toward the bow.
- The cabin's whole after wall (the bulkhead with the companionway) is a vertical plane, not slanted. (A slant seen in the
  profile photo was an illusion of its angle.)
- The cabin top rises toward its after end (profile photo), and its outline seen from the side falls away forward in one
  long slope: the roof's middle rises only a little above the sides at the front, more aft (not a tall crown all along).
- The cabin front rounds down to the deck.
- The cabin's after outboard corners are rounded off down to the coaming: from the cockpit, the after end's outline sweeps
  down from the roof in a big curve to the coaming's top; from the side, the side's top edge bends down into the coaming.
  The coaming itself is unchanged. (Owner's photo from the starboard quarter.)
- The cabin light each side: about 1 m long, from about half a metre aft of the cabin's front edge to just forward of the
  name; its bottom edge straight, its top tapering down toward the bow; rounded ends; a brushed aluminium frame about
  3/4 in wide, screws or rivets all round. The name sits aft of it, clear of the frame.
- The grab rails run most of the cabin top's length, curved to follow the roof (which slants down forward), about
  halfway between the hatch and the roof's edge.
- The jib car tracks are H-shaped, on the cabin top between the grab rail and the roof's edge; cross-bar about 5 in,
  tracks at most 7 in; the car's forward edge level with the grab rail's forward end. (Photo looking aft from the mast.)
- Josephine's jib cars are on the cabin top today. Years ago they were on the side deck: the old track is still there,
  on the side deck beside the cabin's forward window (the owner's photo). Many SF fleet boats moved theirs to the cabin top.
  The exact distances (off the centreline, aft of the forestay) are not yet measured; North Sails' guide gives 58 cm and
  2.65 m for a cabin-top lead, which on our model lands on the grab rail, so one of them is a little off.
- No T-bar or other fitting on the cabin top besides these.
- The cabin may be dark inside by day. Cushions are brown. The mast is keel-stepped. The lantern stays mounted, dark when unlit.
- The companionway hatch slides forward to open, its own length, clearing the opening fully.
- The companionway is closed by three washboards of equal size, varnished wood, which lift out of grooves at the doorway's
  sides and are stowed below (not hinged doors). A small brass keyhole lock, about 3/4 in across, at the top centre of
  the top board. They are a pain to get on and off.
- Opening: the hatch first, then the washboards. Closing: the reverse.

- Midships mooring cleat: on the side deck just inboard of the toerail, halfway along the cabin light (about halfway
  between the hiking rail's forward end and the shroud). Josephine has one, to starboard; the sim fits one each side by
  default (the owner).

## Foredeck
- The mast's foot: a round stainless plate on the deck, a black rubber boot round the mast's foot, and a stainless
  horseshoe: its two ends fixed to the deck either side just forward of the mast, arching up and round the mast's after
  side, about 6 in high at its top aft; the lines thread under it or are made fast to it (owner's photos and words).
- The forehatch: about half a metre forward of the mast on the centreline, square, big enough for a person or the
  anchor (drawn at the class's largest opening, 500 mm, CR 3.31); low, nearly flush with the deck, an aluminium frame
  with rounded corners, a smoked acrylic lid with four aluminium bars running fore and aft, hinged on its forward edge.

## Cockpit
- The lazarettes are solid lockers, not benches.
- The jib winches stand on the side deck about a third of the way along the cockpit, with room for a crew to sit on the
  rail forward of each and another (a little less room) aft of it; their tops about flush with the coaming.
- The winches are fixed, not removable; each is turned from below the side deck, in the cockpit: a spindle down through
  the deck and a crank arm about 10 in long, pointing aft at rest, with a black grip hanging from its end (owner's photo).
- No post under the thwart (that was the boom crutch, only for stowing). The thwart's underside is curved, about 6 in
  deep at the middle tapering to 4 in at the ends. No slanted panels below it.
- Nothing pokes up through the after deck.
