What the flow is telling you

Close-hauled, starboard tack · 12 kt true · apparent 17.6 kt at 28°

Jib trim.ts

  • Angle of attack 18.5° (best 18°): flow attached on both faces.
  • Both luff telltales streaming; the top ones lift first as you point, because the head is twisted 9° open.

The slot illustrative

  • Air squeezed between the jib's leech and the main's lee side speeds up and is turned along the main: the jib's leech sets where the main's luff flow comes from.
  • Over-trim the jib (car forward, sheet hard) and the slot chokes: the main's luff backs (its windward telltales lift) before it's luffing.

Main trim.ts

  • Angle of attack 30° (best 22°): stalling at the leech, 30%.
  • Leech telltales hiding behind the sail: ease the sheet (or traveller down) about 6°.

Under water physics.ts leeway

  • Leeway 3.8°: the keel meets the water at that angle and makes the side force that balances the sails' heel.
  • Pinching (too slow): leeway grows, the keel stalls sooner.

How it's drawn

  • Each sail's section at the chosen height, from the sim's sheet angle, twist, camber and draft.
  • Streamlines: 2D potential flow (vortex panels with the Kutta condition) around those sections in the sim's apparent wind; speed shading from the same flow.
  • Separation: drawn from the sim's own luff/stall amount, not computed (potential flow can't stall).
  • Heights: foot, middle, head; the twist and the wind's gradient change the angles up the sail.
